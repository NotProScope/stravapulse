# Strava Pulse: Pitch Guide (Group 3)

The judges score four things from 1 to 5: **value prop & insights**, **MVP scope & execution**, **technical fluency (Cursor/Grok)**, and **pitch & live demo**. Every section below is built to win one of them.

---

## 1. The one-liner

> **Strava shows you where people run. It doesn't show you who to run with, or where to race. Pulse puts meets, open tracks, run clubs and pace-matched partners on one live map.**

Backup line for Q&A: *"We cut the time from 'I just moved here' to 'my first group run' from weeks to minutes."*

## 2. The problem (Insights score)

### Core pain point (from your Week 8 form, tightened)
People who want to race or train on a track after high school have no central place to find meets, open tracks or training partners. That includes former athletes and complete beginners. The information is scattered across sites only coaches know about, so most people give up before they start.

### Why it's painful *now*: the transition moment
- **High school did it for them.** A coach found the meets, a team gave them partners, and the school owned the track. At 18, all three disappear at once.
- **New city, no map.** College freshmen and new grads relocate. Your notes cite Strava's 2023 Year in Sport report saying 30% of Gen Z athletes relocated and 39% started a new job that year.
- **Campus clubs don't cover everyone.** They're often hard to find and campus-only, which leaves out non-students and people who moved for work.

### Today's Strava, shown in the demo with the Routes toggle
| What they need | What Strava has today |
|---|---|
| "Where can I run on a track tonight?" | Maps shows **routes**: no tracks, hours or crowds |
| "Who runs my pace near me?" | Nothing. Clubs are **feeds**, not meetups |
| "Is there a meet I can just enter?" | The Events list has no pace info, no friends and no map |

### Supporting pain points (pick 2 for one slide; keep the rest for Q&A)
These come from your group notes. The MVP already touches each of them:
- **Hard to find people at your pace** → the pace lens and People layer
- **Clubs are feeds; unclear when or where they meet** → club pins with the next run, pace groups and one-tap RSVP
- **Running alone makes it easy to quit** → wave → match → run together
- **Comparing yourself to faster friends is discouraging** → seeded heats and "12 runners at your pace", so you're never last
- **Safety** → approximate areas only, mutual opt-in, no minors in partner search

### Evidence to collect before pitch day (the biggest point gain available)
Run a 10-minute survey or DM poll with 15–25 first- and second-year students who ran in high school, or want to start:
1. "Since high school, have you raced or done a track workout? If not, why not?"
2. "How would you find a meet or open track near campus today?" (open text)
3. "How long did it take you to find a running group after moving?"
4. "Would you go to a group run if you knew 5+ people ran your pace?" (1–5)

Put one slide of real numbers and 2–3 direct quotes in the deck. Judges score **insights**, and a real survey of your exact persona beats any industry stat.

**⚠️ Verify before using:** "1M+ clubs", "$265M ARR", "~5% pay", and the Gen Z stats come from your notes, which cite Sacra, the5krunner, Business of Apps and Strava's reports. Footnote each one on the slide. Don't present them as Strava's official numbers unless that's what the source is.

## 3. The persona

**Maya, 18, UCLA freshman.** Ran the 400m and 4×400 in high school in the Bay Area. Runs alone around campus at about 10:30/mi. She wants to race again but doesn't know all-comers meets exist, doesn't know Drake Stadium is closed to the public on weekdays, and thinks the run clubs she's seen are "for fast people".

Tell her story in 30 seconds. Then say plainly that the demo runs on the presenter's own Strava profile (Ian: 10:42 easy pace, 8:12 predicted mile, UCLA). The reviewers flagged a mismatch between the pitch persona and the demo account as a credibility risk, so name the switch rather than letting the panel notice it.

## 4. The solution: what makes Pulse different (MVP score)

Show these four ideas, each in under 15 seconds of the demo:

1. **A map built around time, not just place.** A 7-day strip with an event count per day, plus AM / PM. Other maps answer "where". Pulse answers "where, and **when can I show up**". Pins that aren't on that day shrink to dots.
2. **Pace from Strava's own data.** Your easy pace (10:42) and Strava's predicted mile (8:12) seed you into the right heat and the right club pace group. Pins at your pace turn orange, the rest go graphite, and the counts update as you change the lens.
3. **Tracks as live objects.** Each track pin has a tiny lane meter. On the track page you see:
   - "9 at your pace here now"
   - "confirmed open 22 min ago"
   - hours checked against 96 athletes' GPS
   - a workout board where you join a workout, not a stranger

   **Only Strava has this data.**
4. **Plan my first week (the wow moment).** One tap builds a club run tonight, a track workout and a seeded meet, each with a "leave by" time. Add all three and your week draws across the city.

Then the payoff that closes the loop from discovery to belonging:
- the official result stamps onto your activity
- you follow the runners you raced with
- the next meet already has you seeded

## 5. Why Strava wins (why a VC funds it)

- **No cold start.** Strava already has millions of clubs and years of GPS density on every track. Indie apps for finding run clubs or meets die empty. Pulse is full on day one.
- **The data moat.** Live crowd levels and verified hours come free from activity data Strava already collects. A competitor would need sensors or crowdsourcing.
- **Retention where it matters.** Strava earns mostly from subscriptions, so retention is the business. Life transitions are exactly when athletes churn, and Pulse catches them there. With Strava reportedly preparing to go public, retention and growth among young users are the story investors want to hear.
- **New B2B surface.** Meet organizers, run clubs and brands pay for reach. Pulse adds sponsored meets and featured listings to Strava for Business, which already earns from sponsored challenges rather than feed ads, so this fits the model.

## 6. Business model & metrics

| Lever | How |
|---|---|
| **Premium** | Free: see everything on the map. Premium: partner matching, advanced pace lens, crowd forecasts for tomorrow |
| **Organizers** | Free listing; paid "featured meet", registration processing fee, results hosting |
| **Brands** | Sponsored meet series ("[Brand] Winter Open"), pop-ups at meets, first-meet challenges |

**North-star metric:** *time to first group activity* (open Pulse → attend a meet or club run).
**Supporting metrics:**
- 30- and 90-day retention for users who changed city
- Share of RSVPs that turn into a recorded activity at that place
- Organizer listings per city

**Launch plan (matches your advertising notes):**
- Pilot at UCLA in the fall move-in window, seeded with campus clubs plus all-comers series
- Pop-ups at meets
- Signing up for a first meet enters you in a Premium lottery
- Shareable "first meet" result cards for Instagram
- Creator partnerships with track/run-club TikTok

## 8. Live demo script (~2:30)

Open http://localhost:5179 on a laptop (the presenter panel sits beside the phone) or the hosted link on a phone. **Press Shift+R first.**

1. **(0:00) Home.** *"This is my Strava. New card: 5 runs and meets near me tonight."* Tap **Plan my first week**.
2. **(0:15) Two taps.** Pick *"Ran track in high school"*, then *"All of it"*. *"It already knows my pace from my runs: 10:42 easy, and Strava predicts an 8:12 mile."*
3. **(0:30) The wow moment.** Read the three picks out loud:
   - *"A club run tonight, a 4-minute jog away. Their 10:30 group actually ran 10:18 and nobody got dropped."*
   - *"A tempo workout Thursday."*
   - *"Friday's Mile Night, where I'm seeded in Heat 3."*

   Tap **Add 3 to my week**. *"My first group run is tonight. Most people who move take weeks to find one."*
4. **(1:00) The moat.** Tap the SMC track pin. *"Open until 9, 3 of 8 lanes in use, 9 people at my pace there right now, confirmed open 22 minutes ago. That comes from Strava GPS. Nobody else can show this."* Tap **Join Sam's 6 × 400**.
5. **(1:25) The meet.** Open Westside Mile Night. Point at:
   - the prediction and the heat
   - the event schedule
   - "pay at check-in"

   Open the ticket to show the heat sheet.
6. **(1:50) The loop.** Press **F**. *"Friday night: 8:04, official, stamped onto my activity. Then Pulse tells me who I raced with and seeds me into the next meet."*
7. **(2:10) The business.** Press **O**. *"And this is the supply side: 31 of 46 runners found this meet on Pulse. Organizers pay to feature."*

## 9. Q&A prep

| Likely question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where does meet data come from? | Organizers list for free because they want entrants. Seed with USATF all-comers series, college clubs and Strava's existing Events. Results flow back as activities. |
| How do you know if a track is public or busy? | GPS density from Strava activities gives crowd and inferred hours. Athletes confirm with one tap ("verified by 96 athletes"). Schools can claim and edit a listing. |
| Isn't showing partners unsafe? | Opt-in only, off by default. Approximate area, never live location. Mutual wave before chat. No minors in partner search. This builds on the privacy-zone thinking in your notes. |
| Why not a separate app? | The cold start. Strava already has the clubs, the GPS data and the profiles where results land. A standalone app starts at zero. |
| Isn't this just Strava Clubs / Events? | Those are lists and feeds. Pulse is a time-and-pace-aware map tying people, places and events to *when you can show up*, plus registration and results in one flow. |
| How do you make money if it's free? | Pulse protects the subscription base (retention) and opens organizer and brand revenue. Premium gets partner matching and forecasts. |
| What would you build next? | Carpools and after-run recaps (Club Events 2.0), relay team matching, accountability buddy, expanding city by city. |
| Why focus on post–high school? | It's the moment with the most churn and the highest need, and Gen Z is Strava's fastest-growing group. Win them at 18 and they stay through every move after. |

## 10. Technical fluency talking points

The rubric names **Cursor/Grok**, so make that true and visible:
- **Pull this codebase into Cursor** and make 2–3 visible iterations there, such as a new meet in `data.js` or a copy change. Screenshot your Cursor chat for one slide.
- **Tell the AI design-review story.** We ran three AI reviewer personas against the build: a veteran runner who has used every running app, a consumer VC, and a senior PM/designer. They critiqued the build, then critiqued each other's critiques, and we shipped their merged list. That's a strong technical-fluency slide. Examples of what changed:
  - Race math moved from easy pace to Strava predictions.
  - Strangers came off the map.
  - Meets moved to a winter series under the lights.
  - Emoji were replaced with one icon set.
  - The plan-my-week moment, the organizer view and the post-race loop were added.
- **The stack in one breath:** *"Vanilla JS + Leaflet, a canvas-rendered line heatmap, SVG pins that encode type by shape and relevance by color, and every screen driven from one data file."*
- **Know what's real vs. mocked:** demo data, a fixed 6:20 PM demo clock, no real payments. In production these come from Strava's GPS heatmap, Performance Predictions, organizer APIs and FAT timing feeds.
